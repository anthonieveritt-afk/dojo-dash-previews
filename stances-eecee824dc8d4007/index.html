Karate stances (dachi)

Strong stances make strong karate. Here are the stances in the Forza syllabus, with how to stand in each one and the mistake to watch out for.

Reading the pictures: each drawing shows the stance, and the box underneath shows your feet from above, with the front of the dojo at the top. A dotted foot means only the ball of the foot touches the floor.

Beginner stances

The first five stances every Forza white belt learns. Start here.

1

Heisoku dachi

Informal stance

Feet together

How to stand

  • Put your feet together, so your heels and toes touch.
  • Legs straight but not locked. Stand tall.
  • Arms down by your sides.
  • Weight even on both feet.

Common mistake: Leaving a gap between your feet, or slouching. Heels and toes should touch, and you should stand up tall.

2

Musubi dachi

Attention stance

Heels together, toes apart

How to stand

  • Keep your heels together.
  • Open your toes apart, so your feet make a V shape.
  • Stand tall, arms down by your sides.
  • Weight even on both feet.

Common mistake: Letting your heels drift apart. Only your toes open; your heels stay touching.

3

Fudo dachi

Ready stance

Feet apart, fists by your sides

How to stand

  • Step your feet apart, about shoulder-width.
  • Make fists and rest them by your sides.
  • Knees soft, back straight, eyes forward.
  • Weight even on both feet.

Common mistake: Feet too wide or too close together. Aim for about the width of your shoulders, ready to move.

4

Shiko dachi

Sumo stance

Squat with your feet wide

How to stand

  • Step your feet wide apart.
  • Turn your toes out at 45°.
  • Squat down: knees bent and pushed out over your toes.
  • Keep your heels flat and your back straight.
  • Weight even on both feet.

Common mistake: Letting your knees fall inwards or your heels lift. Push your knees out over your toes and keep your heels flat.

5

Kiba dachi

Horse riding stance

As wide as shiko dachi, toes pointing forwards

How to stand

  • Feet as wide as shiko dachi (sumo stance).
  • Point your toes forwards, so the outside edges of your feet are parallel.
  • Bend your knees and push them out, as if you are riding a horse.
  • Back straight. Weight even on both feet.

Common mistake: Letting your toes turn out. That makes it sumo stance. In horse riding stance your toes point forwards.

Next stances

Once the beginner stances feel easy, these two come next.

6

Shomen neko ashi dachi

Cat stance

Front-facing cat stance

How to stand

  • Hips facing the front.
  • A short stance with both knees bent.
  • Back foot turned out at about 45°.
  • Lift your front heel so only the ball of your foot touches the floor, light like a cat’s paw.
  • Most of your weight is on your back leg, so your front leg can lift or turn easily.

Common mistake: Putting weight on the front foot or letting the front heel drop. Sit back on your back leg and keep the front foot light.

7

Zenkutsu dachi

Forward leaning stance

Front knee bent, back leg nearly straight

How to stand

  • Step one foot forwards.
  • Bend your front knee so it is over your toes.
  • Back leg nearly straight, back heel down.
  • Front foot points forwards, back foot points diagonally forwards.
  • A little more weight on your front leg.

Common mistake: Lifting the back heel, or bending the front knee so far it goes past your toes. Keep the back heel down and the knee over the toes.

Yellow belt and above

Junzuki dachi and gyakuzuki dachi are both variations of zenkutsu dachi (forward leaning stance). The difference is which hand punches, and how your hips face.

8

Junzuki dachi

Lead-hand punch stance

A variation of zenkutsu dachi

How to stand

  • This is the stance for a lead-hand (same-side) punch: you punch with the hand on the same side as your front leg.
  • About as long as zenkutsu dachi, but natural, not as long and low as some other styles.
  • Front knee bent over the toes, back leg nearly straight.
  • Hips more square to the front.

Common mistake: Making the stance so long and low that you can’t move quickly. Keep it natural so you can step and punch.

9

Gyakuzuki dachi

Reverse punch stance

A variation of zenkutsu dachi

How to stand

  • This is the stance for a reverse punch: you punch with the hand on the opposite side to your front leg.
  • A little shorter and wider than junzuki dachi.
  • Hips turned, half-facing the front.
  • Front knee bent over the toes.

Common mistake: Standing with your feet on one line, like on a tightrope. Keep a little width between your feet so you stay balanced when your hips turn.
